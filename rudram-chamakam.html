The Eleventh Anuvāka

Taittirīya Saṃhitā · Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · Chamakam, Anuvāka 11
ekā ca me tisraśca me pañca ca me sapta ca me nava ca ma ekādaśa ca me trayodaśa ca me pañcadaśa ca me saptadaśa ca me navadaśa ca ma ekaviṃśatiśca me trayoviṃśatiśca me pañcaviṃśatiśca me saptaviṃśatiśca me navaviṃśatiśca ma ekatriṃśacca me trayastriṃśacca me catasraśca me'ṣṭau ca me dvādaśa ca me ṣoḍaśa ca me viṃśatiśca me caturviṃśatiśca me'ṣṭāviṃśatiśca me dvātriṃśacca me ṣaṭtriṃśacca me catvāriṃśacca me catuścatvāriṃśacca me'ṣṭācatvāriṃśacca me vājaśca prasavaścāpijaśca kratuśca suvaśca mūrdhā ca vyaśniyaścāntyāyanaścāntyaśca bhauvanaśca bhuvanaścādhipatiśca ‖
Odd numbers one through thirty-three, then multiples of four through forty-eight, followed by a closing string of epithets (vigour, generation, continuity, will, the plane of light, the summit, pervasion, the final path, the world, and lordship over it). Tap any number-word to see its card.

This exploration keeps three kinds of claim separate, since they carry very different weight:

Textual — the numbers and their order are directly present in the anuvāka.
Mathematical — the difference-of-squares and adjacent-sum relationships are demonstrable from the numbers themselves, independent of any interpretation.
Traditional — a meaning assigned to a number by commentary, shown here with that attribution rather than as established fact.

A further caution from the sources reviewed: the sequence stops at 33 and 48, not at the natural stopping point the summation pattern would suggest (31 + 33 = 64). Why the text chooses these particular endpoints is an open question, not one the mathematics itself answers. Claims connecting this passage to unrelated modern frameworks (for instance, DNA base-pairing) are speculative and are not presented here as established.
